Problem statement

An environment where classical reinforcement learning runs into systemic limitations.

The agent receives 28 normalized observations: its own position, health, facing direction, the opponent's relative position (if visible), the nearest weapon, wall ray hits, and weapon status. Actions are discrete: forward, strafe, shoot.

The map is generated from a seed: walls, three weapon types (pistol, bow, katana), partial observability via a 72° field of view and ray-casting. A match lasts 20 seconds or until the first kill.

Fitness is a scalar sum of rewards and penalties over the entire episode. This is not a differentiable loss function: inside the simulation — collisions, discrete hits, timeouts.

  • ∂
    No gradient through the environment

    The simulator is written in plain JS: physics, LOS, damage. You cannot call loss.backward().

  • Δt
    Sparse reward

    A kill (+12) and a defeat (−12) happen once per episode. In between — small shaping bonuses for exploration and weapon pickup.

  • ∞
    Coevolution

    The opponent learns too. The fitness landscape is non-stationary: what worked in generation N may fail in N+1.

Why evolutionary strategies

ES treats neural-network weights as a genome and optimizes them via population search — without backprop through the environment.

Criterion Policy Gradient / PPO Evolution Strategies
Environment differentiability Requires a surrogate gradient or a model Not required — only scalar fitness
Sparse rewards Needs careful reward shaping and a value baseline The population averages noise; elites preserve successful mutations
Parallelism Depends on on-policy sampling ~96 independent matches on worker threads
Interpretability Gradients in hidden layers Direct A/B of weights between generations
Network scale Millions of parameters ~2.5K parameters — ES is effective on small MLPs

One generation of the algorithm

  1. Evaluate all 48 genomes in the population in matches against the opposite side's champion (random map seed).
  2. Rank by fitness. Top 8 are elite and pass through unchanged.
  3. Fill the remaining 40 slots: uniform crossover of two elite parents + Gaussian mutation (σ = 0.12, decay ×0.995).
  4. Champion = best genome. Its matches are streamed in the UI and saved to the replay archive.

Blue and red populations evolve independently, but every evaluation match is coevolution: your fitness depends on the opponent's current strength.

Methodological protocol

Measures that separate research from "run it and hope."

A

Random map seed during training

Every eval match uses a new wall layout. The agent learns to generalize navigation, not memorize one maze.

B

Fixed seed for comparison

A/B checkpoint testing (the "Comparison" tab) uses WORLD_SEED = 42 — an identical environment for both contenders.

C

Separating eval and champion stats

Population evaluation matches do not appear in the UI win rate. Main-page statistics reflect only the best genomes' matches.

D

Turn alternation

On even frames blue acts first, on odd frames red does. Reduces "who shot first" bias.

E

Adaptive mutation

If one side wins >60% after 80 decisive matches, the losing side gets a higher σ — accelerating the search for a counter-strategy.

F

Protocol export

CSV generation history and JSON with a full description of the algorithm, architecture, and fitness model — for reproducibility in a report.
